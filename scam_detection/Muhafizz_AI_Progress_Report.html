1. Executive Summary

Muhafizz AI is an intelligent system that automatically detects scam messages and fraudulent phone calls targeting Pakistani citizens. It works across all major languages spoken in Pakistan — English, Roman Urdu, Urdu, and Mixed-language — making it uniquely suited for a country where scam messages arrive in many forms and spellings.

Over the course of the build phase, we developed, trained, and rigorously tested three generations of machine learning models. The final model (V3.1) achieves 96.6% accuracy across 467 independently tested messages that the model had never seen during training. We also built an audio scam-call detection pipeline that converts spoken phone calls into text and classifies them for scam risk — a first-of-its-kind capability for Pakistani telecom fraud.

3. What We Built — Explained Simply

3.1 How the Text Model Works

Think of our model as a trained fraud-detection expert that reads messages the same way a bank security officer would. It looks at combinations of words and character patterns that commonly appear in scam messages — things like "send money now," "your account is blocked," or suspicious phone numbers — and compares them against patterns it learned from 1,123 real examples during training.

The model doesn't just memorize exact phrases. It understands patterns. For example, it knows that "Rs. X ka inaam nikla hai" (you won a prize of Rs. X) is a scam pattern regardless of what number X is, and regardless of whether it's spelled "inaam" or "inam" or "enaam." This is possible because we use both word patterns (sequences of 1-2 words) and character patterns (sequences of 3-5 characters) — the character patterns catch spelling variations.

3.2 Data Augmentation: Teaching the Model from Mistakes

Our key innovation was hard-negative mining — a technique where we specifically create training examples that address the model's weaknesses:

  • Anti-false-positive data: We wrote 82 legitimate messages that sound scammy but aren't — real bank deduction alerts, genuine government notifications, legitimate OTP codes. This teaches the model "not everything about money is a scam."
  • Anti-false-negative data: We wrote 173 scam messages for underrepresented categories — BISP fraud, real estate scams, QR code phishing, tech support impersonation — types the model was previously missing.
  • Roman Urdu spelling coverage: Since Pakistani users type "karna" as "krna," "kerna," "krne," etc., we added spelling normalization rules and training examples for common variants.

The call verdict uses a weighted aggregation algorithm that considers:

  • Strongest signal (35%): The single most scam-like thing said in the entire call
  • Temporal weight (35%): Pressure tactics at the start and end of calls get extra weight
  • Scam density (30%): What fraction of the conversation contained scam patterns

4.3 Smart Preprocessing

Pakistani scam messages use creative spelling and formatting to evade detection. Our preprocessing handles:

  • USSD codes: *786# → normalized token (prevents memorizing specific codes)
  • Roman Urdu spelling: "btayen" → "batayein", "krwayen" → "karwaen" (15+ rules)
  • Phrase normalization: "k badle" → "ke badle", "k sath" → "ke saath" (10+ rules)
  • URL/Email masking: Prevents the model from memorizing specific website addresses
  • Spoken text cleanup: Removes filler words ("uh," "um"), collapses stuttering, handles repeated words

6. Honest Assessment: Limitations & Next Steps

6.1 Known Limitations

1. Dataset Size
Our training set of 1,123 messages is small by deep-learning standards. We compensated with targeted augmentation (hard-negative mining), but the model may struggle with entirely novel scam templates that don't resemble anything in training data.
2. Roman Urdu Spelling Variation
Despite 15+ normalization rules, the extreme informality of Roman Urdu means edge cases persist. Roman Urdu is our lowest-accuracy language at 94.03% (vs. 99.15% for English).
3. Threshold Boundary Sensitivity
Some missed messages cluster in the probability range 0.42–0.48, just below our 0.49 threshold. These represent specific categories (fee-based scams, Urdu phishing, tech support) where the model is less confident.
4. No Transformer Comparison
We did not benchmark against BERT, mBERT, or RoBERTa. TF-IDF + SVM was chosen for speed and interpretability, but a transformer comparison would strengthen the methodology.
5. Audio Pipeline Untested with Real Calls
The call detection pipeline is architecturally complete but has not been tested with real Pakistani scam call audio recordings. Whisper's Roman Urdu speech recognition accuracy is an unknown variable.
6. Static Scam Patterns
Scammers evolve tactics rapidly. The model requires periodic retraining with new scam patterns to maintain accuracy. This is a maintenance consideration, not a flaw in the current build.
